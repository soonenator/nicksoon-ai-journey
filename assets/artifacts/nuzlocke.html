5 Pokémon ROM hacks with built-in Nuzlocke modes

Ranked for your priorities: not brutally difficult, simple to start, and preferably lots of generations. Tap a filter, then expand any card for the tradeoffs.

1
Best match

Pokémon Emerald Crest

Gen 1–9Built-in NuzlockeEasy / Normal / HardEmerald base

The best balance of a huge roster and low friction. It keeps the familiar Emerald adventure, gives you a real Nuzlocke toggle, and lets you stay on Easy/Normal instead of turning the game into a difficulty hack.

Ease
5
Dex breadth
5
Why I’d pick it

All generations 1–9 are represented, and the project’s current site distributes a patch file with simple patching instructions. It also has plenty of optional extras, but you can ignore most of them and play a fairly recognizable Emerald run.

Complexity caveat: there are many optional systems (open world, randomizer, Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, seasons), so resist the temptation to turn everything on for your first run.

2
Simplest Nuzlocke setup

Pokémon Modern Emerald

423 Pokémon totalNuzlocke Easy / Normal / HardcoreVery customizable

Probably the cleanest choice if you want the game itself to enforce the challenge without making bosses absurd. It explicitly includes an Easy Nuzlocke mode plus level caps and lots of quality-of-life switches.

Ease
5
Dex breadth
3
Why I’d pick it

Its difficulty philosophy is specifically not “super hard.” Easy mode lowers opposing levels and boosts experience, while the Nuzlocke menu can enforce permadeath and other rules. The expanded roster is much broader than vanilla Emerald, although it is not a full Gen 1–9 dex.

Best for: your first enforced Nuzlocke if you care more about comfort than having every modern Pokémon.

3
Best open-world option

Pokémon R.O.W.E.

Gen 1–9Built-in NuzlockeEasy / Normal / HardOpen world

Excellent if you want tons of encounter variety and built-in Nuzlocke enforcement. The difficulty can be forgiving; the only reason it is #3 is that choosing your own gym order and route makes the run a little less straightforward.

Ease
4
Dex breadth

Why I’d pick it

Current 2.x-era coverage includes Pokémon through Gen 9 and difficulty settings. The open-world design gives you flexibility to recover from deaths by exploring elsewhere—but it also means more choices about what counts as an area and when to take gyms.

Best for: replayability after you’ve done one simpler Nuzlocke.

4
Best FireRed-style option

Nameless FireRed Project

Pokémon through Gen 6Built-in Nuzlocke optionsLow grindFireRed story

A very good “classic Pokémon, but the game enforces the rules” pick. It has permadeath-style restrictions, encounter clauses, level limits, modern Exp. Share, and a broad Gen 1–6 roster.

Ease
4
Dex breadth
4
Why I’d pick it

Community Nuzlockers have long recommended it specifically for built-in enforcement without Radical Red-level difficulty. It is older than the other picks, though, and the last widely circulated release is from 2017, so expect a few quirks.

Best for: a mostly familiar Kanto story with lots of newer Pokémon.

5
Easiest / most vanilla-like

Pokémon Recharged Emerald

Gen 1–3Built-in NuzlockeEasy modeVery polished QoL

This misses your “many generations” preference, but it may be the least complicated game here. It is basically a polished Emerald with an integrated Nuzlocke, Easy mode, level caps, faster battles, and modern convenience features.

Ease

Dex breadth

Why I’d pick it

The creator distributes a free patch and explicitly lists Nuzlocke and level-cap support. Easy mode removes opponent EVs/IVs and adjusts levels, so the challenge comes from Nuzlocke rules rather than competitive boss design.

Best for: the lowest-stress first run.
